SIMULATION EXTENSION / AGENT CONTROL

Two-axis RGB-D camera control

The simulated camera can look left, right, up and down independently of the torso. RGB and depth share one virtual gimbal at torso XYZ [0.10, 0, 0.42] m; the initial view faces forward.

Agent tool

set_camera_pose(yaw_deg=30, pitch_deg=-20, speed_deg_s=45)

Yaw
−90° to +90° · positive looks left
Pitch
−45° to +45° · positive looks up
Reference
Absolute angles in the torso frame
Speed
1–90°/s per axis · default 45°/s
Reset / cancel
Set both angles to 0 to face forward; stop cancels motion and holds the current camera angles.
Feedback
Reached / cancelled status, current angles and fresh capture timestamp; available to general and legacy agents.

Coordinates stay tied to the image

Every RGB-D observation uses the camera extrinsics at capture time. Camera rotation is applied before points are transformed into the torso frame and the episode-local robot_init frame.

Transform chain
Torso → yaw about +Z → pitch about rotated −Y → camera
Synchronization
RGB, depth and transforms share the same simulation timestamp. A new observation is captured before the tool returns.
Old observations
Cached depth is cleared during rotation. Point sampling and pose conversion reject stale timestamps; reobserve and revalidate pixel targets and arm plans.
Independent frames
Camera rotation does not rotate LiDAR or change the commanded base heading.

Simulation scope. This is a kinematic virtual gimbal, not a factory G1 neck or two additional physical actuators. The body still has 29 controlled joints and 31 actuators including the grippers. Gimbal mass, motor dynamics, backlash and mechanical clearance are not modeled; the existing body animation does not demonstrate camera motion.

Validation. MuJoCo G1/SONIC and HTTP tool smoke tests passed, with checks for depth reprojection, stale timestamps, limits and cancellation. The Isaac adapter is implemented; Isaac runtime and rendering synchronization validation remain pending.
